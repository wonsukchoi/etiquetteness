summary: Avoid giving marigolds (cempasúchil) as a casual gift bouquet; they're specifically associated with Día de los Muertos altars and grave decoration.
context: >
  Marigolds, called cempasúchil (from the Nahuatl cempoalxóchitl,
  roughly "twenty flowers"), are the signature flower of Día de los
  Muertos, believed to guide spirits home to their ofrendas and graves
  with their bright color and strong scent. Giving them outside that
  specific late-October/early-November context reads as an odd,
  funeral-adjacent choice rather than a cheerful gift. White flowers
  carry a similar association the rest of the year, they're the
  standard choice for Mexican funerals, so they're worth avoiding for
  a casual gift too.

Roses, gerberas, or other bright non-marigold flowers are safe defaults for a casual gift. Save marigolds for their intended season and context around Día de los Muertos (late October–early November), when they're expected and meaningful rather than out of place.

As elsewhere, odd numbers of stems are generally the safer choice for a gift bouquet, and white blooms are worth skipping too, they're closely tied to funerals.

If you want a flower with local meaning that isn't funeral-associated, the dahlia is Mexico's national flower and reads as a thoughtful, safe choice for almost any occasion.
