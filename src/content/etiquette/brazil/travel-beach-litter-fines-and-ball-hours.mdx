  Rio's beaches see millions of visitors a year, and litter from
  cans, cigarette butts, and food packaging left in the sand was
  enough of a problem that the city rolled out Lixo Zero (Zero
  Trash), an enforcement program that pairs municipal guards,
  trained sanitation agents, and military police with handheld
  devices to write tickets on the spot. The fine scales with the
  size of the litter rather than being a flat penalty, and it's
  registered against the offender's CPF (the Brazilian equivalent
  of a national ID/tax number), which is also how unpaid fines end
  up blocking things like credit or loan approval later. Tourists
  aren't carved out from any of this.

Pack out anything you bring to the beach, cigarette butts included. Fines scale by volume: roughly R$157 for something can-sized or smaller, up to R$392 for up to a cubic meter, R$980 above that, and R$3,000 for large-scale dumping — a patrol with a handheld ticket device can issue one on the spot.

If you don't have Brazilian ID, ask your hotel or a local contact how a fine would even be processed; enforcement is real regardless, so the safer move is just not testing it.

- **Ball games near the water are also time-restricted** — soccer, frescobol, and frisbee are typically barred right at the waterline between about 10am and 5pm on busier beaches like Copacabana and Ipanema, move further up the sand instead.
- **Skip your own beer and food coolers** — the norm is to buy from the kiosk vendors (barracas) lining the beach rather than bringing your own, which also sidesteps generating litter in the first place.
