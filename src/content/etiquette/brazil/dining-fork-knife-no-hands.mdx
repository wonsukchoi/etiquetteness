title: Keep the fork in your left hand, knife in your right — even for pizza
country: brazil
category: dining
severity: casual
summary: Brazilians eat almost everything with utensils, including pizza and fruit, holding the fork in the left hand and the knife in the right the entire meal rather than switching hands the way Americans often do.
context: >
  Eating with bare hands is generally seen as unhygienic at a Brazilian
  table, so foods that are finger food elsewhere — pizza, sandwiches,
  even fruit served for dessert — routinely get cut and eaten with a
  knife and fork instead. The fork stays in the left hand and the
  knife in the right for the whole meal, continental-style, rather
  than cutting with the right hand and switching the fork over to eat,
  the way American table manners do. It's a small mechanical habit,
  but it's one of the fastest tells that separates a visitor from
  someone who grew up eating this way.

Hold the knife in your right hand and the fork in your left for the whole meal — don't switch the fork to your right hand to eat the way you might elsewhere. If a slice of pizza or a sandwich shows up, expect to see it cut with a knife and fork rather than picked up.

When you're not actively cutting, keep both hands visible above the table with your wrists (not elbows) resting on the edge — hands disappearing into your lap can read as slightly off, not polished.

- **This pairs with the arrive-late norm** — see the entry on hora brasileira for what to expect once you're actually seated and the meal gets underway.
