---
title: Never stand chopsticks upright in rice
country: japan
category: dining
severity: strict
summary: Don't stick your chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice, and don't pass food directly from your chopsticks to someone else's chopsticks.
context: >
  Both gestures echo funeral rites. Upright chopsticks in rice resemble
  incense sticks placed at a Buddhist altar, and chopstick-to-chopstick
  passing mirrors how cremated bones are passed between family members
  at a funeral. Doing either at a meal genuinely unsettles Japanese
  guests — it's not just poor table manners, it reads as a bad omen.
source: Japanese dining etiquette customs
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Rest your chopsticks flat across the top of your bowl or on a chopstick rest (hashioki) between bites — never planted upright in the rice.

If you want to share food, place it on the other person's plate with your chopsticks, or use the opposite (thicker) end of your own chopsticks to serve — don't hand it chopstick-tip to chopstick-tip.
