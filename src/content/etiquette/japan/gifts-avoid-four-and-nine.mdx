  13 carries in the West, and 4 does in Korea for the same
  homophone reason. It shows up beyond gifts too, some hospitals
  skip room numbers 4 and 9, and hotels sometimes skip labeled 4th
  floors, but the rule is most actively enforced around gift
  quantities specifically.

When you're choosing how many of something to give (a set of cups, pieces of fruit, individually wrapped snacks in a box you're assembling yourself), avoid landing on four or nine specifically. Other quantities are fine.

Pre-packaged gift sets sold in Japan are already designed around this, so it mostly comes up if you're building a gift yourself or bringing something from outside the country that happens to come in a set of four.

- **This is the Japanese counterpart to Korea's "avoid sets of four"** — same homophone logic (four/death), with nine added as a second unlucky number specific to Japan.
- **It's most strictly observed with gifts to elders, hospital visits, and formal occasions** — casual gifts among friends are more relaxed about it.
