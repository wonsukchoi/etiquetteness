context: >
  Thailand's lèse-majesté law (Section 112 of the Criminal Code) makes
  defaming, insulting, or threatening the King, Queen, heir apparent,
  or regent a criminal offense, punishable by 3 to 15 years in prison
  per offense. Enforcement has been strict and has included social
  media posts, and reportedly cases where posts made outside Thailand
  before a visitor even entered the country were prosecuted. This is
  the rare entry on this site where the consequence is legal, not
  social. Currency carries the same weight for a related reason:
  since it bears the King's image, stepping on it (feet are considered
  the lowest, least respectful part of the body) or damaging it is
  taken as a direct insult, not simple carelessness.

Avoid any critical, joking, or disparaging comment about the King, Queen, or royal family, in conversation, online, or on social media, including posts made before you arrive in the country. This isn't a soft cultural preference, it's enforceable Thai law, and it's been applied to visitors as well as residents.

Never let a banknote or coin touch the ground and get stepped on, and don't deliberately damage or deface currency, both read as a direct insult to the King's image printed on it. Hand money over and receive it with care, using both hands for anything formal is a safe, respectful default.

- **Stand for the royal anthem** if you're in a cinema before a film starts, or when the national/royal anthem plays in a public space, standing is the expected response, not optional.
- **Don't photograph yourself in a disrespectful pose with a royal image or statue** — treat portraits and monuments of the King with the same seriousness as the person himself.
