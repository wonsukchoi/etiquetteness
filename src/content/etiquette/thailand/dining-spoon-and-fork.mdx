Hold your spoon in your right hand and use it as the primary way food gets to your mouth. The fork stays in your left hand and is only for pushing or guiding food onto the spoon, it doesn't go into your mouth.

Chopsticks come out specifically for noodle dishes, not the default Thai meal. If a shared dish doesn't have its own serving spoon, ask for one rather than reaching in with your personal spoon or fork.

- **Take a generous portion rather than a token one** — leaving a lot of food on your plate can read as a quiet comment that you didn't enjoy the meal, the opposite instinct from cultures where a clean plate signals the host under-served you.
- **Rice is treated with particular respect** — wasting it specifically is seen as more disrespectful than leaving other food behind.
