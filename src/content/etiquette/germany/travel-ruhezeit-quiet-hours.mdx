  Ruhezeit ("quiet time") is a set of nighttime and Sunday quiet
  hours recognized across German municipalities, covering nighttime
  rest (Nachtruhe) and a full day of rest on Sundays and public
  holidays (Sonn- und Feiertagsruhe). It's not a soft social norm,
  a neighbor can and will report a violation to the local public-order
  office (Ordnungsamt), which can issue a fine without going through
  court, commonly starting around €50. The rule catches visitors off
  guard specifically around recycling: the loud clatter of glass
  bottles dropped into a public recycling container is a frequent,
  genuinely reported violation.

Hold off on vacuuming, laundry, drilling, lawn mowing, or loud music between roughly 10pm and 6-7am on weekdays, and for the entire day on Sundays and public holidays, exact hours can vary slightly by municipality, but the general window holds nationwide.

Save glass-bottle recycling for a weekday during normal hours; dropping bottles into a public glass recycling container on a Sunday is one of the most common accidental violations, since the noise is exactly what the rule targets.

- **This isn't limited to apartment living** — it applies broadly enough that a loud weekend renovation project or a Sunday afternoon lawn mower can genuinely draw a neighbor's complaint or a fine.
- **A brief midday quiet window (Mittagsruhe) exists in some areas too**, typically early-to-mid afternoon — check local norms if you're staying somewhere for a while, since this one varies more than the nighttime and Sunday rules.
