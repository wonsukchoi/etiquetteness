  preserving relationships (pakikisama) and saving face are central

  "we'll see," or "maybe" can be a polite refusal, and negative
  feedback is softened or wrapped in praise. A visitor who takes

  criticizes bluntly can damage a relationship that took meetings to
  build. Reading the room and reading between lines is an essential

In Philippine business, a lot is communicated indirectly. A hesitant "yes" or "we can try" often signals "no," and criticism gets wrapped in compliments or delivered to a trusted go-between rather than head-on. Read tone, body language, and reluctance carefully rather than taking every polite word literally.

When you need to give negative feedback, soften it and pair it with genuine positives — or have it conveyed through a trusted liaison. Being blunt might feel efficient, but it can land as a public loss of face, which Filipinos work hard to avoid.

- **"Yes" has shades of meaning** — a firm, repeated yes with enthusiasm is a real yes; a soft or hedged yes likely isn't.
- **Never embarrass a colleague in public**; deliver criticism privately and constructively so no one loses face.
