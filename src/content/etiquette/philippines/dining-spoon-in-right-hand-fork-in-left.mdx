For everyday Filipino meals, the default is a spoon and fork: spoon in the right hand, fork in the left, with the fork nudging rice and viands onto the spoon. This is the reverse habit for many Western visitors, so expect a moment of adjustment. Don't switch to a knife-and-fork setup for a normal meal — it can look like you're signaling the food needs cutting, which it usually doesn't.

When a knife is genuinely needed, it's used to cut, then set down so the spoon can take over. And as a guest, don't feel you must master it flawlessly; making the effort to eat the local way is itself a compliment to the host.

- **Soups and stews** are eaten with the spoon too, sometimes alongside the fork to pick out solid pieces.
- **If you're only given a fork and spoon, that's the intended full setup** — no knife is missing.
