  The jeepney is the Philippines' iconic shared minibus, and it runs
  on a passenger-to-passenger relay system rather than a driver-facing
  one: exact change and cash are passed hand-to-hand down the bench
  until they reach the driver, and any change comes back the same way.
  There are no marked stops on most routes, drivers pull over wherever
  a passenger flags them, so the vehicle genuinely will not stop unless
  someone says "Para po" (please stop) or raps a coin or knuckle against
  the metal roof. Skipping the relay or staying silent when you want off
  isn't just awkward, it visibly marks you as unfamiliar with a system
  everyone else on board takes for granted.

Hand your fare to the passenger seated next to you along with your destination, saying "Bayad po" (here's my fare) — it'll relay hand-to-hand down the bench to the driver, and any change comes back the same way. Have small bills or exact change ready; drivers running an early route especially don't want to break a large bill.

When you're nearing your stop, call out "Para po" clearly, or knock a coin or your knuckle against the roof or handrail so it carries over the engine noise. Signal early, a jeepney needs room to pull over, and calling out right at the corner means you'll be carried past it.

- **Stated seat counts are firm** — if the jeepney is rated for ten, that's ten riders even if it means sitting three-quarters on a bench meant for less; don't expect extra room.
- **Seats nearest the entrance are for seniors, pregnant riders, and people with disabilities** — give them up without being asked if you're seated there when one boards.
