When you enter a Filipino home, greet the elders first and with genuine respect. With older generations, the traditional "mano po" — gently taking the elder's hand and touching it to your forehead — is a deeply appreciated sign of respect (typically with your right hand while slightly bowing). Even without mano, always add "po" and "opo" when speaking to elders.

Before leaving, seek out the elders and the household to say goodbye — don't slip out silently. A warm "paalam po" (goodbye, respectfully) honors the host's home and the people in it, closing the visit with the same courtesy it opened with.

- **Mano is offered with your right hand** while slightly lowering yourself — a sign of deep respect most appreciated by grandparents.
- **Always use "po"/"opo" with elders**, even in casual visits; dropping it reads as careless.
