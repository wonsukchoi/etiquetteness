When a Filipino host lays out a kamayan feast, the food is meant to be eaten with your right hand, no utensils. Push rice and a morsel together with your fingers, lightly form it into a bite, and lift it to your mouth. It's communal and casual, so follow the rhythm of the people around you rather than fretting over technique.

Only eat from the portion of the spread nearest you — reaching across someone else's line of food is the one thing that reads as rude. And if you're not comfortable eating with your hands, don't fake it or make a fuss; a light question to your host about what's expected is far better than refusing the meal's central premise.

- **Kamayan is celebratory** — you're most likely to encounter it at fiestas, family gatherings, and beach parties, less often at formal dinners.
- **Wash your hands before and after** — hosts put out water or a bowl for this; it's part of the ritual, not an afterthought.
