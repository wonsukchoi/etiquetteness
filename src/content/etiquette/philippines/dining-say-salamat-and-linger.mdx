  hospitality (pakikisama) works so hard to build.

When your meal is finished, make a point of thanking the host and, if it's a home-cooked meal, the cook — "salamat po" covers it, and naming the dish you enjoyed is even better. Then stay at the table for a while. Conversation, more tea, or dessert often carries the meal well past the point where the food is gone, and joining in that stretch is part of being a good guest.

Leaving early, especially before the host signals the meal is winding down, can feel abrupt even if you have a reason. If you really must leave, a clear "salamat sa pagkain" (thanks for the meal) and a brief excuse keeps it gracious.

- **Compliment the food specifically** — Filipinos genuinely appreciate hearing what you liked, not just a generic "it was good."
- **Offering to help clear or wash up** is a welcome, low-key gesture at home visits, though hosts often decline.
