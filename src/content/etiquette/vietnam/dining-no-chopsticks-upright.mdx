title: Never leave chopsticks standing upright in your rice
country: vietnam
category: dining
severity: strict
summary: Don't rest your chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice between bites — lay them flat across the bowl or on the rest instead, since two chopsticks standing in rice looks like incense burning for the dead.
context: >
  Two chopsticks planted upright in a bowl closely mirrors how incense
  sticks are placed in rice or sand at a funeral altar or ancestral
  shrine, so the same gesture at a dinner table reads as an unwelcome
  reference to death and mourning, not a harmless place to rest your
  utensils. The taboo shows up across several chopstick-using cultures
  in the region, but it's taken just as seriously in Vietnam as
  anywhere else it's practiced.

When you set your chopsticks down mid-meal, lay them flat across the top of your bowl or plate, or on a chopstick rest if the table has one. It only takes one absent-minded moment of standing them in the rice to make people at the table visibly uncomfortable.

Communal dishes are the norm — expect to serve yourself from shared plates at the center of the table with your own chopsticks, rather than everything arriving pre-portioned.

- **Pass dishes and hand things over with your right hand or both hands, not your left** — the same hand etiquette that governs how you pass food at the table also applies to handing over a business card or a gift.
