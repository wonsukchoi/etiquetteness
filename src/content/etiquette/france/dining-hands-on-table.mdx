---
title: Keep your hands on the table
country: france
category: dining
severity: casual
summary: Rest your wrists on the edge of the table between bites, rather than keeping your hand in your lap, the opposite of common US table manners.
context: >
  In French dining custom, a hand that disappears under the table looks
  evasive. Visible hands (wrists resting on the table edge, not elbows) is
  the polite default, unlike the "one hand in lap" convention taught in
  the US. The custom is often traced back to Louis XIV, who reportedly
  required guests to keep their hands visible at the table, originally so
  no one could hide a weapon in their lap, though today it's pure table
  manners with no security logic left behind it.
source: Mastering French Table Etiquette – French Today
sourceUrl: https://www.frenchtoday.com/blog/french-culture/mastering-french-table-etiquette/
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Keep both wrists resting lightly on the table's edge when you're not actively eating, rather than dropping one hand into your lap between bites.

Elbows still stay off the table, especially while food is being served; it's specifically wrists-visible, elbows-down.

- **Elbows are the one exception people relax on**: in a casual meal it's common to see forearms fully on the table as long as you're still sitting upright, formal settings hold the wrists-only line more strictly.
