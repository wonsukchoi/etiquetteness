summary: Say "Bonjour" (or "Bonsoir" in the evening) before asking a question, entering a shop, or starting any interaction, skipping straight to your request reads as rude, regardless of what language you're speaking.
context: >
  In France the greeting itself is treated as the minimum courtesy that
  opens any exchange. Shopkeepers, waitstaff, and strangers on the street
  expect it before anything else, even before "excuse me" or "do you speak
  English?" The logic isn't fussiness for its own sake: saying bonjour
  acknowledges the other person's presence and dignity before you ask them
  for something, skipping it can come across as treating them as a mere
  service provider rather than a person. Some shopkeepers will visibly
  enforce this, repeating "bonjour" back at you, sometimes more than once,
  instead of answering your question, until you say it too.

Lead with "Bonjour" (daytime) or "Bonsoir" (evening), to shop staff when you walk in, to a waiter before ordering, to a stranger before asking directions. It costs one word and changes how the whole interaction goes.

This holds even if you're about to switch to English, say the greeting in French first, then ask your question in whatever language works.

- **If you forget, don't be surprised if a shopkeeper just says "bonjour" back at you** instead of answering, that's their way of asking you to start over properly.
