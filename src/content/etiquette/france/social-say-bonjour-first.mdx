title: Always say "Bonjour" before anything else
country: france
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Say "Bonjour" (or "Bonsoir" in the evening) before asking a question, entering a shop, or starting any interaction, skipping straight to your request reads as rude, regardless of what language you're speaking.
context: >
  In France the greeting itself is treated as the minimum courtesy that
  opens any exchange. Shopkeepers, waitstaff, and strangers on the
  street expect it before anything else, even before "excuse me" or
  "do you speak English?"
source: French social etiquette customs
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Lead with "Bonjour" (daytime) or "Bonsoir" (evening), to shop staff when you walk in, to a waiter before ordering, to a stranger before asking directions. It costs one word and changes how the whole interaction goes.

This holds even if you're about to switch to English, say the greeting in French first, then ask your question in whatever language works.
