Set your bowl of rice on the left and your soup on the right. Spoon and chopsticks sit to the right of the setting, spoon on the outside.

This isn't just table dressing; the reversed layout (soup left, rice right) is specifically used at Korean memorial services for the deceased. Doing it at a normal meal can unsettle older relatives or hosts, even if younger Koreans today are more relaxed about it.

- **Use the spoon for rice and soup, chopsticks for side dishes** — the two utensils have separate jobs, they're not interchangeable.
- **Never hold the spoon and chopsticks in the same hand at once** — set one down before you pick up the other.
- **Never stand your chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice** — same jesa association as the reversed rice/soup layout, upright chopsticks mirror how the setting is arranged for an ancestor's spirit. Rest them flat across the bowl or on the table instead.
