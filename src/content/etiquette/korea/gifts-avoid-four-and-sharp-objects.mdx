title: Avoid sets of four, knives, and clocks

summary: Don't give gifts in sets of four, and skip knives, scissors, or clocks — all carry death or "cutting off the relationship" associations.
context: >
  The number 4 (사, sa) is a homophone for the Chinese character for
  death, so gifting in fours (four items, four of something) reads as
  a bad omen the same way 13 does in the West — some Korean buildings
  even skip a labeled 4th floor. Knives and scissors symbolize
  severing a relationship, and clocks are linked to counting down
  someone's remaining time, tied to funeral and mortality associations.
  None of these are hard rules for casual friends, but they're the
  kind of thing an older relative or business contact will notice.

Stick to gift quantities other than four — three, five, or seven (seven is traditionally considered lucky) all read as neutral or positive.

Skip knives, scissors, or letter openers as gifts even if they're a nice set — the "cutting off the relationship" association holds even when it's clearly not intended that way. Clocks and watches carry a similar weight, tied to mortality and time running out, so avoid those too.

- **This matters most with elders and business contacts** — close friends your own age are more relaxed about it, but it's safest to default to avoiding all three when you're unsure.
- **If you're unsure whether a gift set comes in fours, check before buying** — packaged goods (towels, glasses, mugs) sometimes default to sets of four in non-Korean markets.
