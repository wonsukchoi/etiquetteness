---
title: Never write a living person's name in red ink
country: korea
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Don't write someone's name in red ink — cards, forms, signatures, anything — it's traditionally reserved for the deceased and reads as wishing them harm.
context: >
  Names of the dead were traditionally recorded in red ink on
  ancestral tablets and funeral registers in Korea (and more broadly
  across East Asia), so using red for a living person's name inverts
  that association directly. It's not limited to formal documents —
  the same superstition applies to something as casual as signing a
  birthday card or writing a name tag. Many older Koreans react
  strongly to it even when they know it was unintentional.
source: Etiquette in South Korea – Wikipedia
sourceUrl: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiquette_in_South_Korea
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Use black or blue ink any time you're writing someone's name, whether it's a card, a form, a nametag, or a signature line next to their name. Red is fine for other uses (highlighting, decoration, correcting your own work) — it's specifically a name that's the issue.

If you're handed a red pen and asked to sign or write near someone's name, switch pens rather than assume it's fine because it's casual or informal.

- **This is one of the more universally known taboos** — even younger, less traditional Koreans who shrug off other superstitions tend to still avoid this one.
- **It extends to your own name too** — don't sign your own name in red either, the association isn't limited to writing about other people.
