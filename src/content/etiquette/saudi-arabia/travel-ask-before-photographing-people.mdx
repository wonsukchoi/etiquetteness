Ask before pointing a camera at anyone, a nod or a spoken "may I?" is enough, and don't press the issue if someone declines. This matters more around women, families, and children than it does with, say, a market stall or a landscape.

Skip photographing government buildings, military areas, police, or security personnel altogether, even if a shot looks harmless from where you're standing, these are treated as restricted subjects rather than judgment calls. Look for posted signage at mosques and heritage sites too, some restrict photography outright.

- **Posting a photo online carries its own risk** — an image that violates someone's privacy or embarrasses them can turn a small in-person lapse into a cybercrime complaint once it's public.
- **Drones need a permit** — flying one in a residential area or a heritage zone without authorization is heavily restricted, not a casual tourist option.
