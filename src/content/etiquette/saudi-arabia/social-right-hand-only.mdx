---
title: Use your right hand for eating, greeting, and giving
country: saudi-arabia
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Use your right hand to eat, shake hands, or pass and receive objects; the left hand is considered unclean and reserved for personal hygiene.
context: >
  This norm is observed broadly across the Arabian Peninsula and traces
  back to the left hand's traditional role in personal hygiene, kept
  strictly separate from eating and greeting. Using the left hand to hand
  someone something, eat, or greet them can be taken as genuinely
  disrespectful, more so than in many other cultures with a similar
  preference. The same body-language hierarchy shows up with feet: they're
  considered the lowest, least clean part of the body, so how you sit
  matters as much as which hand you use.
source: "Saudi Arabian Culture: Etiquette – Cultural Atlas"
sourceUrl: https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/saudi-arabian-culture/saudi-arabian-culture-etiquette
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Eat with your right hand, even when using bread to scoop shared food. Offer and accept things (money, business cards, food, a handshake) with your right hand, or both hands for something formal.

If your right hand is occupied or unavailable, it's fine to briefly explain rather than use your left hand by default.

The same "lowest, cleanest last" logic applies to your feet: don't point them at anyone, rest them on furniture, or cross your legs in a way that shows the sole of your shoe to someone across from you, especially in a mosque or someone's home.
