Listen for a few specific phrases and read them as softened disagreement rather than literal deference: "with respect" (or "with the greatest respect") usually means the opposite is coming, "I'm not fully convinced" means real doubt, and "that could be challenging" often means no.

Watch the intensifiers too, they flip depending on which direction they're modifying: "quite" in front of something positive weakens it ("quite interesting" reads as not that interesting), while "a bit" in front of something negative strengthens it ("a bit concerned" reads as genuinely worried).

- **A longer polite preamble is itself a signal** — the more cushioning before the actual point, the more likely it's a real objection, not just a stylistic warm-up.
- **This isn't unique to business meetings** — the same pattern shows up in everyday British small talk and written communication (emails especially), it's a general register, not a corporate-only one.
