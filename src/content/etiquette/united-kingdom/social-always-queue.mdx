---
title: Always queue, never cut in
country: united-kingdom
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Form an orderly line and wait your turn (at a bus stop, a shop counter, anywhere a group is waiting) and never push in ahead of people who arrived first.
context: >
  Queueing is treated as a basic fairness norm, not just politeness.
  Cutting a queue is one of the most reliably irritating things you can
  do to a British stranger, and people will say something if you do it.
source: British social etiquette customs
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

Look for where the line actually starts before joining (in busy places it's not always obvious) and join at the back, not wherever looks convenient.

If you're unsure whether a queue has formed (a crowded bar, a busy counter), it's normal to ask "is this the queue?" or "who's next?" rather than assuming and pushing forward.
