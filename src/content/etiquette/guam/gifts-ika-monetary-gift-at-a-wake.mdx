Bring an envelope of cash to a bela (wake) or rosary rather than arriving without a gift, ika' is the expected gesture, and it's understood as helping the family with burial costs, not just a token.

Don't worry about matching what other guests give, the amount matters less than the gesture itself, but do expect your contribution to be quietly noted. Chamorro families keep track of who gave what specifically so they can repay the same support later, when your family faces its own loss.

- **This is part of a larger system, chenchule'**, the same reciprocal logic shows up at weddings and fiestas too, so don't be surprised if a Chamorro friend brings a gift to your event expecting nothing immediate in return beyond the relationship itself.
- **If you're unsure how much to give, ask a local friend or host quietly beforehand** — there's no fixed amount, and asking is treated as considerate, not awkward.
