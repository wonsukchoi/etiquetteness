If you come across latte stones, whether at a park, a historic site, or just standing in a field, look, don't touch, and don't climb on them for a photo. They're treated as sacred heritage sites tied to Chamorro ancestors, not ruins to pose on.

Keep your voice down in public spaces generally, loud or confrontational behavior stands out here more than it might elsewhere. Ask before photographing people or their property rather than assuming it's fine, and dress modestly (shoulders, knees, midriff covered) at religious sites or traditional events like a fiesta.

- **This extends to archaeological sites beyond named tourist spots** — latte stones turn up in unmarked places too, treat any you encounter with the same respect regardless of how developed the site looks.
- **A little Chamorro goes a long way** — even just "Håfa Adai" (hello) as a greeting is noticed and appreciated as a sign of respect for the culture.
