---
title: Show deep respect (mamå'lao) to elders
country: guam
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Chamorro culture holds elders in the highest regard — always show deference, don't interrupt, and speak to them with care.
context: >
  Respect for elders is foundational in Chamorro culture. Older people are
  the keepers of family history, language, and tradition, and they command
  automatic deference in any gathering. This shows up in everyday manners:
  not interrupting when they speak, greeting them first, and using warm,
  respectful language. The related idea of mamå'lao is a sense of shame or
  propriety — knowing how to behave so you don't embarrass yourself or your
  family in front of others.
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

When you enter a room of Chamorro elders, greet them and show respect through your posture and words. Wait to be acknowledged before you dominate the conversation, listen without interrupting, and let them lead.

Family reputation matters. A lapse in manners toward an elder reflects not just on you but on your whole family — so when in doubt, err on the side of extra politeness.
