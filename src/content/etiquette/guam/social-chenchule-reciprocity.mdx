summary: In Chamorro culture, when someone gives you a gift or does you a favor, you're expected to return the gesture — the giving keeps going around.
context: >
  Chenchule' is a deep Chamorro tradition of reciprocity, rooted in the core
  value of inafa'maolek (interdependence for the good of the whole rather than
  the individual). It's not tit-for-tat bookkeeping; it's an ongoing cycle
  where gifts, food, labor, and money circulate through family and community,
  most visibly at weddings, births, and funerals. Accepting help or a gift
  carries an unspoken obligation to return it someday — families keep track,
  and the obligation can even pass down to the next generation. Refusing to
  participate, or taking without ever giving back, is seen as self-centered
  and damages trust.

When a Chamorro neighbor or family brings you food, helps with a project, or gives you a gift, receive it graciously and look for a natural way to return the gesture later — a dish you've cooked, help when they need it, or a contribution when they're the ones celebrating.

Chenchule' isn't about keeping score in the moment. The point is that giving never stops; the cycle is what holds families and villages together. The most respected people are those who give generously and remember what they've received.
