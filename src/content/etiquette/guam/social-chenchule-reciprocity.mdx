title: Reciprocate favors with chenchulé
country: guam
category: social
severity: strict
summary: In Chamorro culture, when someone gives you a gift or does you a favor, you're expected to return the gesture — the giving keeps going around.
context: >
  Chenchulé is a deep Chamorro tradition of reciprocity. It's not tit-for-tat
  bookkeeping; it's an ongoing cycle where gifts, food, and favors circulate
  through family and community. Accepting help or a gift carries an unspoken
  obligation to return it someday — not out of debt, but to keep the web of
  relationships strong. Refusing to participate, or taking without ever giving
  back, is seen as self-centered and damages trust.
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

When a Chamorro neighbor or family brings you food, helps with a project, or gives you a gift, receive it graciously and look for a natural way to return the gesture later — a dish you've cooked, help when they need it, or an invitation in return.

Chenchulé isn't about keeping score. The point is that giving never stops; the cycle is what holds families and villages together. The most respected people are those who give generously and remember what they've received.
