summary: In Chamorro tradition on Guam, marriage begins with the families — the man's parents formally visit the woman's parents to ask, and without their agreement the wedding does not go ahead.
context: >
  From the Spanish period up to the recent past, the first stage of a
  Chamorro marriage was the mamaisen saina, a visit to the family elders
  to literally "ask the parents." The prospective groom's parents
  arranged the visit, and if the bride's parents did not agree to it,
  the wedding would not happen. Customarily the bride's family showed
  some reluctance at first, and the groom's parents answered with gifts
  and food spread over several visits. If the parents stood in the way,
  the mañaina (grandparents, aunts, and uncles) took over the family's
  role.

Treat this as the background for why family approval carries weight. If a relationship turns serious, meeting the parents and elders is not a formality, so show them real respect.

Don't expect instant acceptance, either. In the traditional custom the bride's family held back on the first visit, and patience and generosity from the groom's side were part of the process.
