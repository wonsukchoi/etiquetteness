---
title: Never refuse food at a fiesta
country: guam
category: dining
severity: strict
summary: At a Chamorro fiesta, eating what you're offered is part of showing respect — turning down food can offend the host.
context: >
  Fiestas are the heart of Chamorro hospitality. Hosts cook for days and serve
  huge buffets of red rice, kelaguen, barbecue, and more, often feeding the
  whole village. To accept the food is to honor the family's hard work and
  generosity. Refusing, or eating only a token bite, reads as rejecting the
  host's effort. Guests are expected to eat well — and to take food home with
  them, which is itself a sign of belonging.
updatedAt: 2026-09-02
---

When someone hands you a plate at a fiesta, accept it and eat with enthusiasm. Ask for more if you like it. Taking food home (the host usually insists) is normal and welcomed, not greedy.

If you're full, say so warmly but still take a small portion and eat some of it — the gesture matters more than the amount. Never decline the first offer; it's the host's pride on the plate.
