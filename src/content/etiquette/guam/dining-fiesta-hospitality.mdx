title: Let your host offer food more than once
country: guam
category: dining
severity: strict
summary: At a Chamorro fiesta, accepting food the instant it's offered can look eager — the polite move is a token refusal first, then accepting once your host insists.
context: >
  This is a case where two real Chamorro customs pull in the same direction
  but can look contradictory from the outside. Fiestas are the heart of
  Chamorro hospitality — hosts cook for days and serve huge buffets of red
  rice, kelaguen, barbecue, and more, and refusing food outright is a real
  offense. But mamåhlao, the Chamorro code of conduct built on humility and
  not bringing shame to your family, expects you to decline food gently the
  first time it's offered. A proper host offers two or three times before
  accepting is appropriate; accepting on the first offer can read as
  presumptuous. Once you've gone through that back-and-forth, eating well is
  itself a sign of respect for the host's effort. Guests are also expected to
  take food home afterward (called balutan), which hosts insist on as a sign
  of hospitality, not a burden on the guest.

When someone hands you a plate at a fiesta, it's fine — expected, even — to say "oh, I'm okay" the first time. Let them offer again. By the second or third offer, accept graciously and eat with enthusiasm; declining every time, or letting it drag out too long, is its own kind of rude.

If you're genuinely full, say so warmly but still take a small portion and eat some of it. And don't be surprised when your host insists on wrapping food for you to take home (balutan) — accept it. Turning down that offer, not the first plate, is the real faux pas.
