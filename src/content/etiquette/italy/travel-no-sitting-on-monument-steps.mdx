  specifically to enforce it, this isn't a sign easily missed or a
  rule quietly ignored. Venice has followed with its own version,
  banning sitting, lying down, eating, or drinking on designated
  steps, bridges, monument bases, and quays in crowded areas. Both
  cities frame it as landmark preservation, not crowd control, so the
  rule holds even when the steps look empty enough to rest on.

Skip resting on the Spanish Steps in Rome even for a quick photo break, guards patrol with whistles and fines start around $275, rising to about $440 if the marble is damaged. The same logic applies to similar steps, bridges, and monument bases elsewhere, most notably Venice.

Treat any famous stone stairway or historic quay as a walk-through, not a bench, even when locals or other tourists appear to be sitting there. Enforcement varies by moment, but the rule itself doesn't.

- **This isn't limited to sitting** — eating or drinking while perched on a monument, bridge, or quay is covered by the same rules, in Venice specifically it can draw its own separate fine.
- **The renovation history explains the strictness** — a private-funded restoration (Bulgari, 2016) preceded the ban, so authorities treat any wear-and-tear misuse as undoing paid-for preservation work, not just an inconvenience.
