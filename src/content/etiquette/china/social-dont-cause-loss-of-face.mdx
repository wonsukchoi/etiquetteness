---
title: Never criticize someone in front of others
country: china
category: social
severity: strict
summary: Don't correct, contradict, or criticize someone publicly, even gently — it causes them to "lose face" (diu mianzi), a real and lasting social cost, not just embarrassment.
context: >
  Mianzi (面子, "face") is a person's social standing and dignity as
  perceived by others, and it governs a large share of everyday
  Chinese social behavior. Causing someone to lose face in front of
  a group, even through a well-intentioned correction, can damage a
  relationship in a way that's hard to repair, since the harm isn't
  really about the specific criticism, it's about the public nature
  of it. The flip side matters too: praise, generosity, and public
  respect actively "give face" and are a normal way to strengthen a
  relationship.

If you need to correct or disagree with someone, do it privately, one-on-one, rather than in front of a group, colleagues, or family. The same feedback that's fine said quietly can cause real damage said publicly.

Giving compliments, credit, or a generous gesture in front of others has the opposite effect, it's an easy, low-cost way to build goodwill. When in doubt about how direct to be, err toward more private and more indirect than you would elsewhere.

- **This applies to praise for yourself too** — deflecting a compliment rather than accepting it outright is common, since overt self-promotion in front of a group can read as face-seeking at others' expense.
- **Business settings raise the stakes further** — publicly disagreeing with a senior counterpart in a negotiation can stall or end the relationship entirely, not just cause momentary awkwardness.
